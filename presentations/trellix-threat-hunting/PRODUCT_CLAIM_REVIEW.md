# Check the Trellix deck before a customer meeting

Use this worksheet with the [56-slide presentation](Trellix_Customer_Product_Brief.pptx) and its [plain-language guide](README.md). The deck is a conversation aid, not proof that a feature is available, licensed, deployed, or effective in a customer's environment. Record real customer answers and any licensed product details in an approved **private** workspace.

Before presenting, check the exact slide wording and speaker notes against current Trellix documentation or an authorized product specialist. Record the source URL or document version, review date, and any required change to the slide. If a claim cannot be confirmed, remove it from the customer presentation or describe it as a question to verify.

| Slides in the [short route](README.md#a-short-route-through-the-powerpoint) | Review question | What to confirm before saying it to a customer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2–3, executive summary | What business outcome does this slide promise? | Match the language to the customer's stated concern; avoid a guaranteed security outcome. |
| 7–10, device investigation | Which named product, collection method, or response capability is implied? | Current product name, version, supported operating systems, license, permissions, and what evidence the customer's configuration actually provides. |
| 21 and 24, network activity | Does the slide imply packet or network visibility? | Actual sensor placement, permitted capture scope, retention, and whether the customer can see the traffic being discussed. |
| 25–26, threat intelligence and SecondSight | Is the service or feed presented as available in this engagement? | Current offering name, entitlement, coverage, service boundary, and who supplies analysis or escalation. |
| 35 and 40–41, joining clues | Are email, device, and network events shown as correlated? | Which sources exist, time and identity fields, integration status, and a benign comparison. An illustration is not a customer finding. |
| 42–44, customer questions | What information is needed to scope a useful evaluation? | Authorized owner, question, systems and period, exclusions, available evidence, handling rules, and decision maker. |
| 50–52, example evaluation | Is a result or success measure implied? | An agreed harmless or read-only test, observable success criteria, comparison case, gaps, responsible owner, and next decision. |
| 53–55, product references | Are links, names, or versions still current? | Open each primary product reference and replace outdated or separately managed names before distribution. |

## Explain the result simply

In the meeting, say what the tool **might help investigate**, what records are needed, and what must still be tested. For example: “We can evaluate whether the available sign-in and device records answer your question. We will agree on a small test and report what the evidence supports.” Do not say that this lab validates Trellix products or that a customer has received a completed service.

The [customer hunt evaluation worksheet](CUSTOMER_HUNT_EVALUATION_WORKSHEET.md) turns the discussion into a permitted scope and success measure. Keep completed worksheets, account details, screenshots, and customer logs out of this public repository.
